1.1 Three Perspectives on Software Architecture

Core Definitions

IEEE 1471 Standard Definition

The fundamental organization of a system, embodied in its components, their relationships to one another and the environment, and the principles governing its design and evolution.

Decision-Making Perspective

An abstract description of system entities and relationships, involving a series of strategic decision-making processes that are difficult and costly to change later in the lifecycle.

System Concept Perspective

Defines fundamental concepts and properties of a system within its environment, encompassing structural elements, behavioral elements, and their composition into subsystems.

1.2 Six Engineering Purposes of Software Architecture Diagrams:

1. Long-Term System Artifact

Outlasts original creators; languages and developers change, but high-level architecture stays stable.

2. High-Level Decision Making

Provides a bird's-eye view for strategic technical decisions across all project aspects.

3. Team Onboarding

Accelerates ramp-up time for new software developers joining the project.

4. Knowledge Refresh

Helps existing team members reacquaint themselves with modules after working elsewhere.

5. Structural Requirement Changes

Facilitates evaluation when new business or technical requirements mandate structural shifts.

6. Team Alignment

Ensures core components and macro-designs are transparent and shared across the entire team.

1.4 Quality Attributes & Slide Trade-Off Simulator (Blog App)

Trade-Off Analysis

Usability

Complexity experienced by users learning or operating the system.

Maintainability

Ease of fixing bugs or modifying features without destabilizing modules.

Interoperability

Ability to collaborate and exchange data with external systems.

Availability

Degree to which the system remains operational during service windows.

Portability

Ease of transferring software across platforms or OS environments.

Performance & Scale

Responsiveness, throughput, and capacity under varying workloads.

Security & Architectural Compromises

Protection against unauthorized access and data breaches. Just like automotive engineering (where adding armor weight reduces acceleration), improving one quality attribute often requires compromising another.

8. Architectural Patterns vs. Design Patterns Comparison

Exam Comparison Table
Comparison Dimension Architectural Patterns Design Patterns
Definition Fundamental structural organization for a software system Specification assisting in the implementation/refinement of software classes
Scope & Conversion Converts high-level software characteristics into macro system structures Describes specific software units/classes to support detailed coding and construction
Tooling Level Large-level tools: Concerned with large-scale components, global properties, and system-wide mechanisms Small-level tools: Concerned with schemes for refining and building smaller subsystems and classes
Problems Addressed System partitioning, high-level interfaces, scalability, reliability, security Specific micro-level problems in software construction and object interaction
Catalog Examples Layered Architecture, Client-Server, Model-View-Controller (MVC), Pipe & Filter, Blackboard Creational (Factory Method, Singleton), Structural (Adapter, Composite, Facade), Behavioral (Observer, Strategy)
