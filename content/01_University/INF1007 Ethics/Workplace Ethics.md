# Conflict of interest
Something you want for yourself clashes with your responsibility

### Example
You are a investor giving advice to client, you own stocks in XYZ and you recommend that stock to your client without revealing you are holding them. 

A manager promotes a close friend or family member without considering their qualifications or suitability for the position

## Addressing it
- Disclose 
	- Inform supervisor about potential conflict of interest
- Evaluate
	- Assess the extent of conflict and determine if involvement might compromise fairness
- Mitigate
	- Recuse yourself from decision-making process to avoid undue influence
- Seek Guidance
	- Consult company code of conduct or ethics committee

# Bribery
When someone offer, gives or receive something of value, to influence the action of the other party

### Example
Company executive offering sum of money to government in exchange for securing lucrative contract

Different culture many have a different interpretation of the purpose of a small gift

University Policy
- Gifts below AUD50, no declaration needed


# Whistle Blowing
Act of reporting wrongdoing, unethical behavior, or illegal activities within organization by an insider or employee who has access to sensitive information.
Whistle blowers *may be punished* for the action, Lose their job or chances of advancement.

### Example
[The Challenger Explosion]
Failure due to O-ring near Rocket Booster, Roger Boisjoly an employee at Morton Thiokol who makes the rocket boosters repeatedly raised concerns internally within Morton Thiokol to delay launch until weather conditions improved.

## People become whistle-blowers for different reasons
Good Motive - Help public
Questionable Motive - Exact Revenge, Avoid a severe punishment

## Types of response to Whistle Blowing
- Corporate Response
	- Whistle-blowers are disloyal
	- Has many harm
		- Bad publicity
		- Makes it hard for people to work as team
		- If company causes harm, public can use legal remedies to seek damages
- Whistle Blowing as an organizational failure
	- Emphasizes the need for creating management structures and communication processes that allow concerns to be raised, discussed and resolved (Is this realistic ?)
- Whistle-blowing as Moral Duty 
- (If you answer YES to the first 3 you have the RIGHT to whistle-blow)
- (If you answer YES to all 5 you have the DUTY to whistle-blow)
	- Is serious harm to the public at stake
	- Have you told your manager
	- Have you tried every possible channel
	- Do you have persuasive documented evidence
	- Are you sure whistle-blowing will work

# Improving Corporate Ethics by an ethics Program

## Corporate Social Responsibility
Organization should act ethically by taking responsibility for the impact of its actions on the environment, the community, and welfare of its employees

## Business Ethics
Refers to principles, values, and moral guidelines that guide the conduct of individuals and organizations in the business context

# Advantages of promoting a work environment that encourages good business ethics

## Goodwill and Customer Loyalty
Strong reputation for ethical conduct can attract and retain customers, enhance brand loyalty, and create a competitive advantage in the market

## Employee Engagement and Retention
Employees more likely to be engaged and committed when they work for an organization that operates ethically and aligns with their own values

## Protecting the Organization from Legal Actions
Vicarious liability, or imputed liability, is legal rule that holds a person or company responsible for actions committed by their employees. Promoting ethical behavior reduces the likelihood of misconduct in the first place.
