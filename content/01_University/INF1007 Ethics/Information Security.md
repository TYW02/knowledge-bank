# What is Information Security
Practice of protecting information by mitigating information risks.
- Typically involves reducing probability and impact of unauthorized access, use, disclosure, disruption... of data and information

## CIA
- Confidentiality
- Integrity
- Availability

## Threats to Information Security
- Malware
- Vulnerabilities in software and systems
- Unsecured wireless networks
- Phishing and social engineering
- Insider Threats
- Physical threats
- Data breaches
- Denial of Service attacks

### Human element is weakest link in IS
- Lack of information security awareness
- Human error
- Bring Your Own Device (BYOD) practices

### Important for organizations to offer
- Education and training programs
- Regular security awareness campaigns

## Relevant Laws and Regulations
- Cybersecurity Act
	- Framework for regulation of critical information infrastructure(CII) in Singapore
- Computer Misuse Act (CMA)
	- Criminalizes various cyber activities, including unauthorizes access, hacking, and computer-related offenses.
- Telecommunications Act
	- Regulates the telecommunications industry in Singapore
- Official Secrets Act (OSA)
	- Aimed at protecting official information related to government and public agencies.
- Copyright Act
	- Protects intellectual property rights, including software and digital content.




# Hacker Ethics
Set of values and beliefs that guide behavior of hackers.
Principles of hackers ethics:
- Sharing
- Openness
- Decentralization
- Free access to computers
- World Improvement

## Types of Hackers
- White Hat (Ethical Hackers): Authorized professionals who legally hack to *find and fix* system vulnerabilities
- Black Hat (Malicious Actors): Cybercriminals who illegally infiltrate systems to steal data, deploy ransomware, or cause damage for financial/political gain
- Gray Hat (Boundary Testers): Hackers who access without permission but without malicious intent. Often find flaws and demand a reward.



# Ethical Dilemmas

## Information Security as Credence Good
Search Goods: Attributes that can be evaluated prior to purchase. 
Experience Goods: Goods that can be accurately evaluated only after the product has been purchased and experienced.
Credence Goods: Goods that are difficult or impossible to evaluate even after consumption has occurred.

## Ethical Hacking
Involve attempts to hack into an organization's systems to uncover vulnerabilities.

Ethical hackers must first obtain proper authorization to perform their hacking activities.

### Ethical Dilemmas & Issues
Ethical hackers may come across confidential or sensitive information during their assessments. They should handle such information with care, maintain confidentiality, and not disclose it to unauthorized individuals or misuse it for personal gain.

#### Relevant ACM Code of Ethics:
- 1.2 Avoid Harm
- 1.6 Respect Privacy
- 1.7 Honor Confidentiality

## Disclosing vulnerability can be a complex ethical dilemma
2 General approaches:
### Responsible Disclosure
Reporting vulnerability to relevant parties (vendor or software developer) before making it public or sharing it with unauthorized individuals.

### Full Disclosure
Involves making the vulnerability information available to the public, including potential attackers.

#### ACM Code of Ethics:
- 1.2 Avoid Harm
- 2.1 Strive to achieve high quality in both the processes and products of professional work
- 2.9 Design and implement systems that are robustly and usably secure.

> Full disclosure may have legal consequences
