# The need to Communicate
Human, consciously and unconsciously, are always striving to **make sense of our surrounding world**.

Symbols help people understand that world: *Gestures*, *Signs*, *Objects*, *Signals* and *Words*
- They provide communication methods to understanding experiences by conveying recognizable meanings that are shared by societies.
- Symbols are **material culture** and can also convey **non-material** cultural meaning.

Besides written and spoken words, (non-verbal) *body language* and *sign language* are used to communicate.

## Language and Symbols
Language is a system that uses symbols to #Communicate and transmit culture.

*Letters*, *Pictographs*, and *hand gestures* are all symbols that forms a language.

To communicate, people require intimate knowledge not only of, the alphabet but also the word that was formed and the meaning of the word. Taken together with other words, they convey a specific meaning.

Cultures tend to borrow words from another language to convey a (new) concept

> Language is **constantly evolving** as societies create and adopt new ideas.

# Sapir-Whorf Hypothesis
> Language shapes our perception of reality and our behavior
> - Edward Sapir & Benjamin Whorf

The hypothesis is based on the idea that people experience their world *through their language*, and therefore understand their world through the cultural meanings **embedded** in their language.

> In other word, language shapes **thought** and thus **behavior**
### For Example
In Western Culture, the number 13 is associated with bad luck while in Oriental culture, the number 4 is considered unlucky

The hypothesis has been interpreted to suggest that if a word **does not** exist in a language, then users of that language *cannot have* the experience. They will interpret or associate the concept with another word.

# Social Risk for AI in Communication
Many AI applications carry *Safety*, *Security* and *Privacy* as well as *Social Risk*. These risk are not independent and may not be exclusive to AI.

For technology to be socially acceptable they must:
- Consider **long-term impacts** on future generations
- Worry about **social cohesion**, in particular employment, resource sharing, inclusion and social recognition
- Integrate the **imperatives of human rights** as well as cultural and ethical values of a community
- Consider **global constrains** affecting the environment or international relations.

Some social risks for AI in communication are:
- Transparency and Bias
- Behavior Manipulation
- Deepfake


## Transparency and Bias
AI transparency refers to how possible it is to understand the inner working of a system.

Algorithm Level:
- Complex AI systems process humanly unmanageable amounts of data in humanly incomprehensible ways, resulting in **unknown biases** in the results.
- Codes and data sets, which are essential for accountability, *may not be open to scrutiny*

Interaction Level:
- As AI systems get more complex and advanced, so are their *ability to interact* with the users as well as *influence* their shared environment.
- Ability of AI systems to *learn and adapt* to their users bring forth an entirely different human-AI interaction paradigm towards transparency where *human interact and collaborate with AI* leading to better outcome and increase his trust in the system.

Social Level:
- Consumers are required to deal with individual **privacy agreement** of respective systems, leading in information overload.
- Experts have proposed a collective approach which is more beneficial to society, whereby trust is formed in the system *as a whole* and individual parts are highly regulated by law.


> AI systems can be intentionally designed as unbalanced for propaganda or commercial goals

Users *may not be explicitly warned* about the underlying objectives of systems that may distort their outcomes.
The problem is more pronounced when there are **hidden and non-intentional biases**

## Behavior Manipulation
Manipulation:
The act of influencing or controlling someone or something in a skillful or devious way, often with an intent to deceive or gain an advantage.
- Seen as a potential violation to a person's rights to self-determination
- When individuals are manipulated, their *thought*, *emotions* and *perceptions* are being controlled or influenced by others, often without their knowledge or consent


Social Media platforms have been accused of using algorithms to manipulate what content users see in their feeds, in order to promote certain political or commercial interest.
- Filter bubbles: Determine the content users see and allow advertisers to target specific groups of users with tailored advertising. Creating a filter where users are **only exposed to selected information and ideas.**
- Fake Accounts: Used to spread misinformation, **amplify certain voices** or **create the illusion** of widespread support for a particular idea.
- Content Moderation: Rely on automated content moderation to identify and remove harmful or inappropriate content. However risks manipulation to **censor certain voices or ideas.**
- Amplification of extreme or sensational content: Tend to amplify content that is extreme or sensational, as they generate the most engagement and attention. Creates an environment *where misinformation and harmful content spread rapidly*

Microtargeting has been criticized for using personal data to tailor advertisement to individuals and influencing their choices, raise concerns about manipulation of political and consumer behavior.

Personalized search algorithm have been criticized for their potential to reinforce existing biases, limit diversity of information and create filter bubbles.


Personalized search algorithm rely on large amount of data about each user to function
effectively. These data can be used to build a detailed profile of each user, including
their interests, preferences and behavior patterns


## Deepfake
Can be used to spread **misinformation**, create **fake evidence** for legal or political purposes, and **invade privacy**

Exacerbate existing social and political division by creating fake narratives or spread propaganda.

### Types of Deepfake
- Photo Deepfake: Face and body replaced or blended with another person's face
- Audio Deepfake: Person's voice may be replaced by another by imitating or have written text translated into audio of different voices and different accents
- Video Deepfake: Person's face may be swapped with another person or modified to take on another look

> Deepfake and fake news present serious challenges to society as "seeing is believing" and "a picture is worth a thousand words"


Biggest vulnerability remains with humans who may be subjected to:
- Validity effect: Individuals tend to trust fake news after repeated exposures
- Confirmation Bias: News confirms someone's preexisting beliefs
- Desirability Bias: Individuals will believe if it pleases them
- Bandwagon effect: Peer pressure or fear of missing out take control of someone's perception and behavior.

# How Does Learning Occur ?

#Learning theories can be categorized into behavioral, cognitive and constructive.

**Behaviorism** equates learning with *changes* in either the form or frequency of **observable performance**. Learning is accomplished when a *proper response* is demonstrated following the presentation of a specific stimulus.

Primary concern is how association between stimulus and response is **made, strengthened and maintained**. Learning is characterized as being *reactive to conditions in the environment* as opposed to taking an active role in discovering the environment.

**Cognitivism** stress on the **acquisition of knowledge** and internal mental structures. Learning is equated with *discrete changes between states of knowledge* rather than with the changes in response.
> Main focus of cognitivism is on how information is received, organized, stored and retrieved by the mind. Learning is not so much what learners do but with what they know and how they come to acquire it.

**Constructivism** equates learning with creating meaning from experience. The mind filters input from the world to produce its own unique reality.
> Human **create meaning** as opposed to acquiring it. Learners do not transfer knowledge but build their personal interpretations of the world based on **experiences and interactions**

Internal representation of knowledge in constructivism is constantly open to change. Knowledge emerges in contexts within which it is relevant.

## Metaphors of Learning

**Acquisition Metaphor** (AM): Human mind as a container that can be **filled by knowledge** (concept, idea, meaning) and the learner is the owner of these knowledge.
> Teacher may help student acquire knowledge by *teaching, coaching*. That is placing knowledge into the learner's container.
> 
> Once acquired, knowledge can be applied, transferred to a different context and shared with others.
> Knowledge can be **passively passed** to another person or can be actively constructed by the learner.


**Participation Metaphor** (PM): Learning as a process of **becoming a member** of a certain community. Learner, while actively involved in its activities, will speak and act *according to the norm* of the community
> Learner becomes part of the community, the they affect and inform each other. 
> Learning takes place as a result of interaction with others in a context and the world.


### Examples
Traditional classroom fits well with AM where student acquire knowledge from teacher and there is assumption that what is learned can be applied in life.

Apprenticeship fits well to PM, apprentice must negotiate the meaning and practices of the trade as he take part and contribute to company's activities. Transfer is less important as the learner is already in the context.

AM brings forth *"The learning paradox"*: How can we want to acquire a knowledge of something that is not yet known to us ?

**Accounting for transfer of knowledge** is difficult in PM. Implementing PM in a learning setting like school is difficult while its more possible for workplace learning.

Hence, both approaches are needed to *complement one another.*

## Do we need a 3rd metaphor of learning ?
AM is focused on acquisition of *"ready-made" knowledge* rather than on creation of something new. PM is focused on *passing knowledge* and practices from one generation to another without substantial or deliberate changes or transformation.

Both are **inadequate** for dealing with innovative, expansive or progressive aspects of knowledge advancement.

The knowledge creation metaphor conceptualize learning and knowledge advancement as
*collaborative processes* for developing shared objects of activity. These objects or
artefacts can be defined as knowledge, ideas, practices and materials.

▪ The interaction among different forms of knowledge or between knowledge and other
activities are requirement for this kind of innovateness in learning and knowledge creation.
▪ The focus here is not on the certainty of knowledge but how knowledge is used and how
it is developed.


# Why do People Play ?
#Play can be understood through *physical*, *psychological* and *sociological* reasons.

**Physical** explanation of play suggest that people have surplus of energy that need to be worked off.
People evolved to be more efficient in meeting their survival needs, they have time left for play, exercise and bond.

Play can be a form of *relaxation* and *rejuvenation*, as contrast to work. **Work** drains a person but **play** energizes the tired, distracted or dispirited.
Playing also **shape behavior** through *practice and learning* which is useful for living in complicated, changing environment.

**Psychology** explanation argues that play is rooted in the emotion of pleasure.

**Imaginary** is one key domain of play. All players fictionalize reality and explore the
possibilities of these settings. This is apparent in *expression of art and literature*, as
well as in *video games.*

Play is central to a **person’s development** because play is the laboratory where one
*exercise and refine their abilities* to comprehend and manage the world. Children play
because they enjoy acts of control, from bodily movement to their surrounding

People play, and learn, by *interacting with one another*, by setting and responding to
challenges. **Rules** are important to coordinate such actions as well as the *imaginative*
*pretense*, both individually and collectively.

### 4 Forms of Play
- Games of competition (agon): PvP, contest of *physical ability, skill or wits*
- Game of Chance (Alea): Centers around *randomness* and indeterminacy (roulette)
- Game of Simulation (Mimicry): Players taking up *another identity* in make-believe, performances and role play
- Vertiginous games (Ilinx): Create joyful sense of *disorientation* and disequilibrium like roller coaster

In each form **constraints** must be imposed to avoid "Corruption"
- Unrestrained agon gives rise to brutality, violence and greed in order to win that calls for **rules and referee**
- Alea without limit becomes **superstition** where desire to evade and placate destiny by watching for signs and omens.
- Mimicy may end in alienation where extreme identification leaves the player *no original self to return to*
- Unbridled ilinx is as visible as drug and alcohol use where *temporary disruption becomes dependency*

- Rules heighten the *pleasure of winning* by creating “ideal conditions” where there is equality
of fair play and thus heighten the uncertainty of who will win.
▪ Superstition is an expression to renounce personal effort. However, *minimal effort is required*
to search for the “signs” and placate the “gods”. Players do experience the drive of alea without
the need for superstition due to the indeterminacy of fate.
▪ Mimicry must not allow a player to *identify too deeply* but provide continued indeterminacy
of make-believe.
▪ Games can be as potent as drug and alcohol in producing *forms of disorientation* where the
player transits in and out.

Each form of play has a tendency towards indeterminacy and uncertainty. Agôn and
alea give up some range for indeterminacy that heightens pleasure while mimicry and
ilinx give up some depth. It is through element of uncertainty that makes it possible for
play to organise their “pure” form.

Serious play is viewed as satisfying and rewarding and considers the play
experience as important as any outcomes produced. Therefore, serious play is a
process of immersion with the player’s attention fully engrossed and absorbed,
resulting in significant learning.

Through studies, serious play has
been shown to aid cognitive development of new insights, the expression of tacit
knowledge, facilitation of analogical reasoning in strategy, communication of new
insights, development of shared understanding and ethical norms.
Example: Lego Serious Play

# Technology and Smart Toys
Increase in accessibility and decrease in cost leads to an exponential rise in all things electronic. However, technological toys **have not proven** themselves to be more educational, more efficient or more entertaining.

Smart toys cost less to produce than they once did, and the flashy features make them easier to sell.

Smart toys **require** as software ecosystem which can be **updated** and **customized**

Most agree that smart toys are highly connected applications which uses *sensors*, and they can be *upgraded or connected* to the internet

Smart toys can **share data and receive updates**. Smart toys are **customizable and adaptive**, fundamentally *changing the nature of play* and how players interact, not only with the toys but with one another.

## Evolution of Toys
Toys stopped being tools for play and became integral part of play

An early version of smart toy have the capacity to *adapt* to the *abilities or actions of the player.*
Example: Character in game will only thrive when the player care, feed and interact with it, and they will inform the player how were they doing.

While toys cannot relate to a player as well as humans can, they provide *undivided attention* to the player, which could be mistaken for intimacy, especially in children. Parents risk abdicating the bond with children to a digital nanny.

## Under the Hood of Smart Toys
Software can learn and adapt over time, and increasingly games are less passive and more interactive.

3 distinct feedback loops:
1. **Real-time adjustment** of behavior based on player's reaction
2. **Pooled behavior and automated learning**, where smart toy get smarter by *considering all* players' feedback and adaption
3. Product roadmaps established by **analytics**. Building analytics into toys **allow real and faster feedback**. In turn, new functionality can be pushed out quicker

## Future of Play
Most going to be shaped by *data and technology*

Plays becomes **connected** where players *extend their game* through the Internet with other players *at a distance*.

Smart toys will be truly smart with Artificial Intelligence. With **adaptive AI**, non-player character can *change their behaviors* based on what has been learned from the human's previous actions.

More plays happens in *immersive* environment brought about by virtual reality. 

Innovation speeds up with *leading-edge manufacturing* products like 3D printers and microcontroller boards which are cheap, light, customizable and easily available. This *maker approach* to toys and use of online sales and distribution platforms are accelerating the pace of innovation in the toys industry,
