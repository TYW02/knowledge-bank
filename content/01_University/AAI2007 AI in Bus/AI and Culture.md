#Culture
# What is Culture ?

A Society is a group of people sharing a community and culture

Culture describes the shared values, beliefs, norms, language, practices, and artifacts of these people, and includes material and nonmaterial elements.

Culture can be material or nonmaterial
- Material culture are items you can touch / tangible
- Nonmaterial culture consists of ideas, attitudes, and beliefs of a society. Intangible

Cultures may vary but share a common element. Culture universal are patterns or traits that are globally common to all societies.


# Ethnocentrism
Cultural differences are far more prevalent than cultural universals.

Ethnocentrism is a belief or attitude that one's own culture is better than all others.
- Believing your own culture is the correct measuring standard 
- Almost everyone is a little bit ethnocentric
- Can lead to disdain or dislike of other cultures

People from one culture could see another as uneducated, backward, or even inferior

**Cultural imperialism** is the deliberate imposition of one's own cultural values on another culture.

## Cultural Relativism
- Practice of assessing a culture by its own standards rather than viewing it through the lens of one's own culture.
- Requires an open mind and willingness to consider, and even adapt to, new values, norms and practices

## Xenocentrism
- Opposite of ethnocentrism refers to the belief that another culture is superior to one's own


# Values and Beliefs
#Values
Values refers to ideas, principles and standards members of culture hold in high regard
- Deeply embedded and critical for learning a culture's beliefs
- Suggest how people should behave, but don't accurately reflect how people do behave
- Portray ideal culture, standards society would like to embrace and live up to.

Sanctions are forms of social control, ways to encourage conformity to cultural norms

Social control encourages most people to conform regardless of whether authority figures are present.
Values can change over time and between groups as people evaluate, debate and change collective social beliefs. Values also vary from culture to culture.

# Norm
Behaviors that reflect compliance with what cultures and societies have defined as good, right and important.

Formal Norms:
- Established, written rules existing in all societies
- Enforced to varying degrees

Informal Norms:
- Casual behaviors that are generally and widely conformed to
- Dictate behaviors without the need of written rules, and so maybe difficult to learn when one is new

Norms may be either *mores* or *folkways*

*Mores* are norms that embody the moral views and principles of a group
- Often have religious foundation and violating them can have serious consequences

*Folkway* are norms without any moral underpinnings
- Direct appropriate behaviors in practice and expression
- Folkway in 1 culture could be extremely rude in another
- Act without thinking in order to get seamlessly through daily routines, without analyzing every action
- Learned by observation and imitated

# Cultural Change
2 broad categories of changes are innovation and diffusion (to spread out)

Inventions may shape a culture by replacing older ways of carrying out tasks, being integrated into current practices, or creating new activities. Their adoption reflects cultural values, and their use may introduce new norm and practices.

Material culture tends to diffuse more quickly than nonmaterial culture.

*Culture lag* refers to time that passes between introduction of new item of material culture and its social acceptance.

*Diffusion* relates to process of integration of cultures into mainstream Idea concepts, or artifacts.

# Hofstede's Dimensions of Culture

## Power Distance
Extent to which the less powerful members of organizations and institutions accept and expect that power is distributed unequally.
- Represents **inequality** but defined from below not from above
All societies are unequal, but some are more unequal than others

## Uncertainty Avoidance
A society's tolerance for ambiguity. To what extent a culture programs it members to feel uncomfortable or comfortable in **unstructured situations** (novel, unknown, surprising, and different from usual)

Uncertainty avoiding cultures try to minimize possibility of such situation by strict behavioral codes, laws and rules

Uncertainty avoiding countries are more emotional and motivated by inner nervous energy.
Uncertainty accepting cultures are more tolerant of opinions different from what they are used to; they try to have fewer rules.

## Individualism vs Collectivism
Describes the degree to which people in society are integrated into groups

Ties between individuals in individualist cultures are loose; everyone is expected to look
after him/herself and his/her immediate family only

People in collectivist cultures are integrated into strong, cohesive groups from birth;
extended families offer protection in exchange of unquestioning loyalty.

## Masculinity vs Femineity
Refers to distribution of values between genders as a society

A masculine society is assertive and competitive while a feminine society is modest
and caring

## Long Term vs Short Term Orientation
Refers to degree a society links its own past when dealing with the challenges of the present and future

Values associated with long-term orientation are perseverance, thrift, ordering
relationships by status, and having a sense of shame.

Values associated with short-
term orientation are reciprocating social obligations, respect for tradition, protecting
one's "face", and personal steadiness and stability

## Indulgence vs Restraint
Indulgence stands for a society that allows relatively free gratification of basic and
natural human desires related to enjoying life and having fun.

Restraint stands for a society that controls gratification of needs and regulates it by
means of strict social norms
