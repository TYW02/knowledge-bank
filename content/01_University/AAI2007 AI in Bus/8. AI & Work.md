# 3 Industrial Revolution

## Before Industrial Revolution
- Humans moved from foraging to farming, and domestication of animals
- Animals used for production, transportation and communication

## 1st Industrial Revolution (1760-1840)
- Triggered by steam and water powered machine, railroad enabling mechanical production
- **Craftsmen to unskilled labour** operating machines.

## 2nd Industrial Revolution (1870-1914)
- Triggered by electricity (oil & gas) and assembly line enabling mass production
- Factories, automobiles, telephone and telegraph
- Increasingly professional skilled labour, introduction of white collared jobs

## 3rd Industrial Revolution (1947-1990)
- Triggered by semiconductors, computers and Internet
- Robotic technologies transforming manufacturing, reducing need for human power
- Synthesis, critical thinking, analytic skills favoured.

# 4th Industrial Revolution (1947-)
- Triggered by fusion of technologies and interaction across physical, digital and biological domains. Enabling automation and complex data exchange.
- **Smart Manufacturing**: Production facilities and logistics systems without human intervention
- **Knowledge economy**: Product and service based on knowledge intensive activities
- **Digital Platforms VS pipelines**: Aid creation of value by producers in their network, VS creating product and service through a linear value chain

## Impact of 4IR on Business
- Many industries using technologies to create new ways of serving existing needs, significantly disrupting existing value chains.
- Innovative competitors can replace well-established ones quicker by improving *quality*, *speed* or *price*
- Growing transparency: Consumer engagement and patterns force companies to adapt how they *design*, *market* and *deliver* products and service
- Technology allows sharing or on-demand economy
- Lowering barriers for businesses and individuals to create wealth
- Personal and professional environments of workers are altered.

> Impacting customer expectations
> Product enhancement
> Collaborative innovation
> How businesses are organized and operate


# Customer Expectations
- Shift from customer expectations to **customer experiences**
- Shift from demographic segmentation to potential customers based on their willingness to share data and interact
- Multiple sources of data offers granular insights to customer's purchasing journey
- More data in supply chain enables customers more comparison on the performance and price of products hence shifting power to consumers.
- Companies are required to respond in real time wherever they or their customers are.


## Data-Enhanced Products
Products and service are enhanced with digital capabilities that increase their value
- Data and analytics are transforming **maintenance** through monitoring and proactive maintenance
- Ability to predict performance of asset provides insights on operational tolerances and provide the basis for outsourcing non-core products.
- Ability to predict performance of asset also made possible to pay service providers by actual rather than expected performance.

## Collaborative Innovation
- Companies share resources through collaborative innovation, significant value can be created for all parties.
- Potentially spawn new business models which bring businesses from multiple industries to provide an integrated business experience
- Companies need to think about how **offline** and **online** worlds can work together in practice.

# New Operating Models
- Companies need to operate faster with greater agility
- Digital platform now intimately connects to physical world
- Shift focus on selling products to delivering services
- Consumer shift from owning physical objects to accessing digital services.
- Data-powered businesses unlocks insights
- Invest on security of their digital systems.
- Talent and culture must be upgraded and evolve for new skill requirements
- Organizational structures need to balance employee motivation and communication with business priorities.


# AI-enabled Automation
Automation uses technology to reduce human intervention
- Muscle power
- Mechanical power
- Assembly line to enable mass production
- Robots
- Smart machines


# 3 Kinds of human capabilities that cannot be sustainably substituted by AI
- Developing creative interventions from mistakes or motivated by emotions
- Exercising judgement or manifesting "deep" creativity or strategy
- Modelling tacit knowledge

> [Tacit Knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacit_knowledge): Experience based on "know-how" 

## Learning from mistakes and emotions
Many great innovation discovered by accident
AI **unable** to uncover valuable new techniques by mistake
**Boredom and fear** are powerful motivators to develop new techniques

## Judgement and Deep Creativity
Human can exercise judgement that is not (currently) possible for machine, especially when there is conflicting data.
Human can **create connection** "out of nothing"

# Why and When to use AI Automation
Most obvious value is outsourcing complex or mentally draining repetitive tasks.
Narrow, well-defined and does not require much flexibility

## Four Data Scenarios

### Known Known
Information and data are accessible and reliable.
As more high-quality data is fed, predictive ability increases.
**Machines tend to outperform human**

### Known Unknown
Information and data not readily accessible but when available, they are reliable.
Lack of comparable data makes it difficult for machine to deliver prediction and form framework.
**Human intuition and judgement are better predictor than machines**

### Unknown Unknown
Information and data not accessible nor reliable.
Something new or unexpected takes place which bears only some similarity to the past, hence, little or not data exists.
**Here both human and machine struggles**

### Unknown Known
Information and data are accessible but not reliable hence unable to be used by machine.
Results are almost guaranteed to be biased unless the exact nature of bias can be understood, then humans may interfere and attempt to correct the basis.
**Machine are biased**

# 5 School of Thoughts

## Dystopian & Utopian

### Dystopian 
Man & Machine will face off struggle for existence which machine will win

AI systems take on tasks at core of middle and high skill jobs, robots will perform menial work that requires low skill labour
There will be massive unemployment, falling wages and economic dislocation.

### Utopian
Machine will take on more work, but result will be economic growth instead of decline.

Advancement in AI will enable machines to emulate the working of human brain in its entirety
Human memory and knowledge can be downloaded to computers, replicated human brain will do most of the cognitive work while robots will do all the heavy lifting.

Little human labour is required but universal income can be given out

## Technology Optimist & Productivity Sceptic

### Technology Optimist
See burst of productivity, but not captured in official data
When companies learnt to take full advantage of AI, leap in productivity will spur economic growth and living standards.

Economic growth will not be distributed evenly, many jobs will be displaced.
Investment in technology must be coupled by investment in education and training.

### Productivity Sceptics
Gains in productivity levels will be low despite use of machines

Advanced economies facing aging population and other issues will see stagnant growth

### Optimistic Realist
Machines can spur productivity gains that match previous technological waves

Productivity will advance rapidly in certain sectors and companies while new jobs will be created.
Demand for high and low skill workers, whose jobs could be easily automated, will rise while demand for middle skill workers fell.
