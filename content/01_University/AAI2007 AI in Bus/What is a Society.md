#Society
The term society has roots from Latin words, *societas* (fellowship, association, alliance, union, community) and *socius* (companion, ally)

Most definitions of society surround similar ideas
> "A society is a group of individuals involved in persistent **social interaction**, or a large social group sharing the **same spatial or social territory**, typically subject to the same political authority and **dominant cultural expectation**"
> \- Wikipedia 

> "A society is a group of people who live in a **particular territory**, are subject to a common system of **political authority**, and are aware of having a **distinct identity** from other groups around them. Some societies, like those of hunters and gatherers, are very small, numbering no more than a few dozen people."
>  \- Giddens, 1993

Humans and animals form societies to seek *stability*, *continuity*, *security and predictability*, often by defending or creating *spatial belonging*, border demarcations and collective memories anchored in particular places.


# Human Societies
Sociologists generally classify societies by their level of *industrialization*
- Preindustrial societies depends largely on human labor
	- **Hunter-gather societies** relied on their surroundings for survival where they *hunt and forage*.
	- **Pastoral societies** relied on the *domestication of animals* for food, clothing and transportation.
	- **Horticultural Societies** formed where rainfall and other conditions were favorable to *grow stable crops*. They did not have to abandon their location to follow resources unlike nomads
	- **Agricultural Societies** emerged when *farming* became possible by new technologies. Farmers learned to use metal tools, rotate crops and reuse waste products which led to better harvests and surpluses. Settlements grew into *towns and cities* and regional centers of trade and commerce were formed.
	- **Feudal Societies** have *strict hierarchical system of power* based around *land ownership and protection*. Land were owned by the nobles but cultivated by peasants. In return for working the land, peasants were guaranteed a safe place to live. Power was handed down through family lines.

**Industrial Societies** formed when Europe experienced a dramatic rise in technology ushering in an era known as the *Industrial Revolution*
- Increased productivity and technology give rise to *urban centres*. 
- People less interested in working the land and moved to cities.
- People more focused on *acquiring wealth and achieving upward mobility*

**Post-industrial societies** are also known as information societies or digital societies where the focus is on the *production of information and services*, shifting away from the production of goods
- Power lies with those in charge of storing and distributing information hence technical skills become the means for success

# Technology and Society
Technology has allowed humans to progress from a *hunter-gather society* to an *agrarian society*. Similarly, technology is one key driver behind an **industrial society** and an **information society**


# Social Issues
#Social_Issues
A social issue or social problem is any condition or behavior that has negative consequences for large number of people and that is generally recognized as a condition or behavior that needs to be addressed.
- Objective: must have negative consequence for large numbers of people
- Subjective: must be perceived as a need to be addressed
- When is a social issue a social issue ?

### Example
- Poverty
- Discrimination against race, religion, gender and sexual orientation
- Aging
- Substance abuse and addiction
- Population and environment related like pollution, climate change, food scarcity

# Societal Issues in Singapore
 3 Social issues Singapore strive to overcome as a Nation, as espoused in the National Pledge.
 - Racial and religious harmony
 - Inclusivity and social progress
 - Social justice and equality


# Racial and Religious Harmony
Maintaining racial and religious harmony remains a significant issue today and for the foreseeable future so long as Singapore is made up of a *diverse demography*

Singapore's *immigrant-friendly policies* also mean that there is a constant stream of migrants who will bring along racial and religious identities from where they came from

Singapore government take a *pre-emptive and interventionist approach* to maintain social order, in 3 ways.
- Multicultural policy where instead of a single national culture, multiculturalism acknowledge, embraces and emphasizes the importance of the diversity of the various groups to achieve a cohesive society. Multiculturalism, together with meritocracy, help build a fair system for all.
- Legal instruments provides the necessary means to prosecute individuals who have overstepped the boundaries and threaten Singapore's fragile social make-up
- Encouraging intercultural interaction takes a softer approach in educating and fostering the appreciation of racial and religious harmony


### Examples of multicultural policy
▪ *Presidential Council of Minority Right*s to check against any laws which discriminate against
any race, religion or community.
▪ Given the special place of Malay Muslim in Singapore, the *Administration of Muslim Law Act*
give power and jurisdiction to the *Islamic Religious Council of Singapore*, Syariah Court and
Registry of Muslim Marriages.
▪ Ethnic-based self-help groups (Mendaki, Sinda, Eurasian Association) ensure that minority
groups *do not lag behind* and are given support in the form of culturally sensitive ethnic-based
help.
▪ Group Representative Constituency (GRC) ensure minority representation in policy making.

### Examples of legal instrument
▪ Article 15 of the Constitution of Singapore guarantees the freedom of religion. However, any
action while practicing one’s religion may not be contrary to Singapore’s laws as sovereignty,
integrity and unity of the nation take precedence.
▪ The Sedition Act considers words promoting feelings of ill-will and hostility between races
and classes to be seditious.
▪ The Internal Security Act allows the “preventive detention” of a person who act in a manner
prejudice to the security of the country, public order or essential services.

### Examples of intercultural integration
▪ Education for diversity starts in the formative years of schooling (CCE, Racial Harmony Day)
▪ Community Engagement Programme (CEP) ensure community preparedness for crises
which may undermine racial and religious trust.
▪ Inter-Religious Organisation (IRO) aims to inculcate the spirit of friendship and cooperation
among the leaders and followers of different religions.
▪ Inter-Racial and Religious Confidence Circle (IRCC) tries to engage religious and community
groups at the neighbourhood level

# Inclusivity and Social Progress
Civil society organisations play an important role in promoting *inclusivity and social progress*

These organisations attend to or provide social services to the needy and disadvantaged in society. However, at the same time, some of these organisations seek to change policy, legislation or the worldview of the public and the government.

Civil activism result in a more socially inclusive and compassionate Singapore where common space and sense of identity and pride grows.


# Social Justice and Equality
Tommy Koh opined that the Singapore society is both socially just and not socially just.

### Why Tommy Koh thinks that Singapore is a socially just society
- There is **no discrimination against women** in Singapore. Women have achieved equality with men in many areas but under-represented in politics and boards of directors
- There is **no discrimination against persons based on their race, colour, language or religion**
- All Singaporeans have access to employment, housing and healthcare
- The rule of law is strong, and judiciary is independent and impartial
- Meritocracy is a pillar or Singapore's governing philosophy
- All Children have **opportunity to attend a good school**


### Why Tommy Koh thinks that Singapore is not socially just 
- There is a **growing inequality of income and wealth**. The Gini coefficient of Singapore is 0.41 in 2013. Gini coefficient is the measure of income inequality.
- Many hardworking Singaporeans are not paid a living wage which is sufficient to maintain himself and his family in material sufficiency and to live with dignity. Singapore has no official definition of poverty line; a quarter of our workforce earned less than $2,000 per month in 20131.
▪ Singapore does not have a **minimum wage**.
▪ Many school children go to school with no pocket money for lunch. Since 2000, the Straits Times
School Pocket Money Fund has disbursed about $42million to 128,000 needy children.

▪ Tommy Koh suggested that to address social injustice, issues related to **income distribution**
must be tackled.
