## What is Gross Profit
How much money the company made off of total revenue after subtracting the costs of the raw goods and labor used to make the goods.

- Can be used to calculate company's gross profit margin
	- Gross Profit / Total Revenue = Gross Profit Margin

### Look for those with consistently higher gross profit margins
- E.g. Coca-Cola -> 60%
- Moody's -> 73%
- Burlington Northern Santa Fe Railway -> 61%
- Wrigley Co. -> 51%

### Companies that have poor long-term economics
- United Airlines -> 14%
- General Motors -> 21%
- US Steel -> 17%
- Goodyear Tire -> 20%


### However in the tech world this might not be true
- Microsoft -> 79%
- Apple -> 33%


### Having high gross profit margins
Is the company's durable competitive advantage which allows freedom to price products and service it sells well in excess to cost of goods.


# General rule of thumb

- Companies with gross profit margins of 40% or more tend to be companies with some sort of durable competitive advantage.
- Companies below 40% tend to be companies in highly competitive industries where competition is hurting overall profit margins
- Any gross profit margins of 20% is below is a good indicator of a fiercely competitive industry
