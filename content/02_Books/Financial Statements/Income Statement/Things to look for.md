
# Cost of goods sold

OR
- Cost of materials and labor used in manufacturing the product it is selling

(The lower the better)


# Gross Profit 
#KeyNumbers

## Gross Profit / Total Revenue = Gross Profit Margin
- Gross profit $7,000  / Revenue $10,000 = Gross Profit Margin 70%
- Gross profit is how much money the company made off of total revenue after subtracting the costs of the raw goods and the labor used to make the goods.

- Companies that have excellent long-term economics working in their favor tend to have consistently higher gross profit margins than those that don't.

## General Rule (Not fail-safe)
### Look into the past 10 years to ensure consistency
- Companies with gross profit margins of 40% or better tend to be companies with some sort of durable competitive advantage.
- Any gross profit margin of 20% and below is usually a good indicator of a fiercely competitive industry, where no one company can create a sustainable competitive advantage over the competition.



# Operating Expenses
- All the company's hard costs associated with research and development of new products, selling and administrative costs of getting the product to market, depreciation and amortization, restructuring and impairment charges, and the catch-all "other" that include all non-operating, non-recurring expenses.

# Selling, General & Admin
## There should be consistency here
- Costs for direct and indirect selling expenses and all general and administrative expenses incurred during the accounting period.
	- Includes management salaries, advertising, travel costs, legal fees, commissions, all payroll costs, and the like.

### Lower SGA the better, and if they can stay consistently low even better
- In the world of business anything under 30% is considered fantastic.
- If we see a company that is repetitively showing SGA expenses close to, or in excess of, 100% we are probably dealing with a company in a highly competitve industry where no one entity has a sustainable competitive advantage.
